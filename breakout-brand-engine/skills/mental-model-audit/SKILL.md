# Mental Model Audit

People do not evaluate your product. They slot it into a model they already
have, and the model does most of the work. AG1 succeeded partly because
"foundational nutrition" let people file a subscription under
*infrastructure* rather than under *another supplement*. Same powder,
different mental shelf, very different price tolerance.

Before you write creative, find out which shelf you are landing on by
default — because the default is rarely the one you want.

## Run the audit

**1. Collect the raw beliefs.** Pull from review sections, forum threads,
support tickets, sales call recordings, and comment replies on competitor
ads. You are looking for sentences that start with "I always thought…",
"isn't it just…", "my doctor said…", "I stopped because…".

**2. Cluster into models.** Most categories have three to five live models.
For daily health products they tend to be some version of:

- *Insurance* — I take it so nothing goes wrong. Low engagement, high churn
  risk, price-sensitive.
- *Optimisation* — I take it to perform better. High engagement, evidence-hungry.
- *Repair* — I take it because something is wrong. Urgent, and the most
  dangerous model in regulated categories because it invites disease claims.
- *Ritual* — I take it because it's who I am now. Highest retention, hardest
  to earn.

**3. Locate your default.** Show three real ads to people cold and ask which
shelf they'd file the product on. The gap between that answer and your
intended model is your actual creative problem.

**4. Decide: borrow or break.**

| Move | When it works | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Borrow the model | Model is favourable and widely held | You inherit its ceiling and its objections |
| Break the model | Model caps your price or invites the wrong claims | Expensive; needs repetition and proof |

Breaking a model is a multi-quarter brand investment, not a campaign. Budget
it that way or don't attempt it.

## Watch the regulated edge

The *repair* model is where compliance risk enters through the customer's
head rather than through your copy. If people file a supplement under
"fixes my condition", ordinary language starts reading as a disease claim —
and regulators assess the **net impression** a reasonable consumer takes,
not just literal wording. Auditing the model is therefore a compliance
activity, not only a marketing one. Route findings into `health-claim-gate`.

## Output

Produce a one-page model map: the live models, which one you currently land
on, which you're targeting, the three beliefs you must borrow, and the one
belief you must break — with the proof that breaking it requires.

Keep it to one page. A model map nobody rereads is a model map that stops
governing the work by week three.
