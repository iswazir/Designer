# Objection Ladder

A high click-through rate with weak conversion almost never means the ad was
bad. It means the ad answered doubt one and the buyer had five. Objections
arrive in a fixed order, and each rung needs a different asset, a different
length, and often a different voice.

Treating objections as a ladder rather than a list is what stops teams from
answering the same easy doubt forty different ways while the expensive doubt
goes untouched.

## The rungs

1. **Relevance** — "Is this for someone like me?" Answered in the first
   second, by casting and context, not copy.
2. **Comprehension** — "What actually is it?" One clear sentence. Most
   premium brands lose people here by leading with mood.
3. **Credibility** — "Why should I believe that?" Evidence, specificity,
   third-party signal. In regulated categories this rung carries the most
   legal weight — route the language through `health-claim-gate`.
4. **Comparison** — "Why not the cheaper one, or the one I already use?"
   Requires naming the alternative honestly, including doing nothing.
5. **Risk** — "What happens if I'm wrong?" Guarantee, cancellation terms,
   how easy it is to stop.
6. **Timing** — "Why now and not later?" The weakest rung to lean on and the
   most abused; discounting here trains customers to wait.

## Map assets to rungs

Build a grid: rungs down the side, funnel stages across the top. Every cell
should name an owning asset. Empty cells are where money leaks.

A common and expensive pattern: fifteen assets on relevance, nothing on
comparison. The fix is not more volume, it's redistribution — which is a
capacity decision as much as a creative one, so reconcile it with
`tentpole-allocation` before committing editor time.

## Find the live objection

Don't guess the rung you're losing on. Three cheap reads:

- **Drop-off shape** — high CTR, low add-to-cart points at comprehension or
  credibility; high add-to-cart, low checkout points at risk or comparison.
- **Search behaviour on site** — what people type into your own search bar is
  the objection in their words.
- **Cancellation reasons** — for subscription products, the stated reason for
  leaving is usually the objection that was never really answered, only
  postponed.

## Write to the rung

Each rung has a native register. Relevance is visual and fast. Credibility is
specific and calm — numbers, mechanisms, named evidence. Risk is plain and
almost administrative; flourish here reads as evasion.

The most common craft error is answering a credibility objection with brand
tone. When someone asks why they should believe you, atmosphere sounds like
a dodge. Hand these lines to `claim-to-copy`, which is built for exactly
this problem.
