# Category Creation

Competing inside an established category means inheriting its price ceiling,
its objections, and its comparison set. Creating one means the comparison set
is yours to define. Olipop and Poppi did not win the soda aisle by being
better soda; they built "modern soda" and made the old aisle the alternative.

Category creation is expensive and slow, and most brands attempting it are
really doing positioning. Know which one you are doing before you spend.

## When it's warranted

All four should be true. Fewer than four means position sharply within the
existing category instead.

- The existing category's frame caps what you can charge or claim
- Your product genuinely differs in kind, not degree
- No incumbent owns the new frame yet
- You can fund repetition for several quarters

## Naming

A category name is not a tagline. It has different jobs: it must be sayable
by a customer to another customer, it must classify rather than persuade, and
it must be broad enough to hold more than your one product.

Test candidates on three criteria:

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| Repeatable | Would a customer say this word unprompted? |
| Classifying | Does it tell someone what shelf this is on? |
| Extensible | Can it hold your next three products? |

Names that fail *repeatable* are the most common trap: they are precise,
internally beloved, and nobody says them aloud.

## Building the frame

A category exists when three things are true in the customer's head: it has a
name, it has a *boundary* (what is not in it), and it has a *default
alternative* it displaces. Most brands do the first and skip the other two,
which is why their category never quite forms.

Say what you are displacing. Naming the alternative honestly is what makes
the new frame legible — and it does more work than any amount of describing
yourself.

## The repetition burden

Categories form through consistency, not cleverness. The same words, the same
boundary, across every channel and every quarter, long past the point the
internal team is bored. Internal boredom typically arrives at about 10% of
external awareness — plan for the pressure to change it just as it starts
working.

Codify the language through `voice-codification` so it survives contact with
freelancers and agencies, and audit shipped work against it monthly.

## In regulated categories

A new category name must not itself imply a health benefit you cannot
substantiate. Names that classify by *what the product is* are safe; names
that classify by *what it does to your body* invite a claims review of the
category name itself, which is a difficult position to unwind after launch.
Clear the name through `health-claim-gate` before external use.
