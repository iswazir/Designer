# Distinctive Assets

Advertising that people enjoy and misattribute to a competitor is a loss with
a good aftertaste. Distinctive assets are the non-verbal codes — colour,
shape, sound, character, motion, framing — that make an asset yours in the
fraction of a second before comprehension starts.

At volume this stops being a branding nicety and becomes an operating
control: codes are what let thousands of assets from dozens of makers read as
one brand.

## Build the inventory

List every candidate code and score each on two axes, honestly:

- **Fame** — what share of the audience links it to you when shown alone?
- **Uniqueness** — what share links it to *only* you?

High fame with low uniqueness is a category code, not a distinctive asset;
you're paying to advertise your competitors. High uniqueness with low fame is
an *investable* asset — the thing to commit to and repeat.

Test with the code shown in isolation, brand name removed. Testing it inside
a finished ad measures the ad, not the code.

## Which codes to build

Prefer codes that survive compression, cropping, muting and speed. In
practice that favours:

- **Colour** used at scale rather than as an accent
- **Shape and silhouette** — recognisable at thumbnail size
- **A character or recurring figure** — the highest-retention code available,
  and the reason mascot brands punch above their spend
- **Sound** — the most under-built code in DTC, and the one that works while
  the viewer is scrolling past
- **A structural device** — a consistent edit rhythm, framing, or transition

Typography and logo placement matter but are the weakest codes in feed
environments, because they require the viewer to already be looking.

## Enforce without flattening

Codes should be mandatory; execution should not be. The productive rule is a
floor, not a template: every asset carries at least two codes, and one must
be non-verbal. That constrains attribution while leaving editors room to make
genuinely different work.

Write the floor into `concept-brief` under must-include, and check it during
review as a binary — codes present or absent — rather than as a taste
judgement. Binary checks can be delegated; taste judgements cannot, which
matters when you are trying not to be the bottleneck. See
`review-delegation-gates`.

## Across channels

Out-of-home and brand film are where codes are built, because scale and
duration let a code register. Paid social is where they are cashed in. Judge
a code's OOH execution on whether it is recognisable at 70mph with no copy —
if it needs the logo to work, it is not yet an asset.

## Review cadence

Re-measure fame and uniqueness annually. Codes decay when competitors adopt
them, and the correct response is usually to deepen commitment rather than
to switch — switching resets the clock on the only thing that compounds.
