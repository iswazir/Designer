# Asset Multiplication

Multiplication is decided before the camera turns on, not in the edit. A day
with an athlete produces either forty assets or four hundred depending on
whether the derivative plan existed that morning. Red Bull's advantage was
never a bigger budget — it was treating every shoot as an inventory event.

The discipline is simple and consistently skipped: write the downstream
manifest first, then shoot to fill it.

## Write the manifest before the shoot

For each production, list every downstream use with its spec. This becomes a
shot list obligation, not a wish list.

- **Paid social** — ratios, durations, hook variants, with and without talent
  speaking, silent-legible versions
- **Organic** — behind-the-scenes, unpolished takes, vertical native moments
- **CRM** — stills with copy space, sequence-appropriate moments
- **OOH** — high-resolution stills, extreme crops, motion loops for digital
- **Retail and events** — loops, silent cuts, portrait and landscape
- **PR** — clean stills without brand codes, quotes captured on camera
- **Web** — hero motion, ambient loops

## Shoot for the manifest

Four habits produce most of the multiplication:

1. **Capture coverage deliberately.** Wide, medium, tight and insert on every
   setup, even when the hero shot is obvious. Crops you cannot reframe are
   assets you don't have.
2. **Shoot silence and speech separately.** Most feed environments are muted;
   a version that works silent effectively doubles usable inventory.
3. **Leave negative space.** Copy space in-frame is the difference between a
   still that works in six placements and one that works in one.
4. **Roll between setups.** The unguarded moments between takes are the
   organic and PR material, and they cannot be recreated later.

## Multiply without duplicating

The failure mode at volume is hundreds of near-identical assets that fatigue
simultaneously — a bigger library that behaves like a small one. Genuine
multiplication varies a *structural* dimension, not a cosmetic one.

| Structural (real variety) | Cosmetic (fake variety) |
|---|---|
| Different hook family | Different headline wording |
| Different entry-point moment | Different colour grade |
| Different objection answered | Different music bed |
| Different narrative order | Different end-card |

Cosmetic variants have a place — they refresh a proven winner cheaply — but
counting them as new concepts is how a testing roadmap quietly stops
learning. See `performance-creative-ladder`.

## Rights govern the ceiling

Talent contracts constrain territory, duration, channel and sometimes format,
and those constraints must be attached to the asset as metadata at creation.
An unlabelled asset whose usage window expired is a legal exposure sitting in
a library that nobody can audit. Encode rights expiry in the naming
convention so the system can find them.

## Post-shoot triage

Within 48 hours, cut the manifest's highest-priority items and log everything
else against the manifest with gaps marked. Gaps identified in the first week
can often be filled with a pickup day; gaps found in month two cannot.
