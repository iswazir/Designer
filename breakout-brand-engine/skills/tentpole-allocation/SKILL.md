# Tentpole Allocation

Every content team discovers the same failure the same way: a tentpole
arrives, everyone is pulled onto it, always-on performance creative goes
stale for six weeks, and paid efficiency degrades right as spend peaks behind
the launch. The tentpole looks successful and the quarter isn't.

Allocation is the mechanism that prevents this, and it has to be decided
before the tentpole, because during it nobody will defend always-on.

## Three claims on capacity

- **Always-on** — continuous performance creative. Demand is roughly
  constant and set by fatigue rates, not by ambition.
- **Tentpole** — launches, brand film, OOH, seasonal moments. Lumpy,
  scheduled, highly visible.
- **Fixed windows** — talent and ambassador shoots. Immovable, and the
  scarcest input in the system.

Fixed windows are constraints, not priorities: they do not compete for
capacity, they *define* the calendar everything else fits around.

## Set a floor before you plan

Calculate the always-on floor from the fatigue curve: how many structurally
distinct new concepts per month does the account need to avoid decay? That
number is a floor, not a target, and it is protected capacity.

Plan tentpoles with whatever remains. If a tentpole cannot fit above the
floor, the honest options are to shrink its scope, buy external capacity, or
move it — not to quietly borrow from always-on and discover the cost in
week five.

## Reserve for reality

Three reserves that repeatedly justify themselves:

1. **Reactive reserve** — a slice held for cultural moments. Without it,
   every reactive opportunity becomes an emergency that displaces planned
   work at the worst possible moment.
2. **Rework reserve** — realistic revision rounds, based on your actual rework
   rate rather than the optimistic one. Rework is capacity you have already
   spent; pretending otherwise just moves the shortfall later.
3. **Derivative reserve** — post-shoot multiplication is real work and gets
   systematically underestimated because the shoot feels like the deliverable.
   See `asset-multiplication`.

## Make trade-offs visible

Publish allocation as a single view: capacity, committed, reserved, free.
When a new request arrives, the conversation becomes "what comes out"
rather than "can you fit this in" — the second question has only one socially
acceptable answer and it is how teams end up overcommitted by 40%.

Bring the same view to planning with media and brand partners so the
trade-off is a shared decision rather than a content-team problem.

## Signals you are over-allocated

Rework rate climbing, brief quality falling, always-on concept count drifting
below floor, and editors reporting they have not made anything they are proud
of this month. The last one is the earliest signal and the easiest to
dismiss — it usually precedes the metrics by a full cycle. See
`editor-pod-design`.
