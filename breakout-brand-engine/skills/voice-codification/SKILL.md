# Voice Codification

A voice held in the heads of three people is not a voice, it is a
bottleneck. The test of codification is blunt: can a freelancer who has never
met you write a line that a founder would approve, on the first try, at 2am,
without asking?

Oatly's voice survives because it is built from reusable rules — the aside,
the self-aware admission, the refusal of category grammar — not from a mood
board. Rules travel. Moods do not.

## What an executable voice guide contains

Adjectives ("bold, warm, human") are the most common and least useful
artifact. Replace them with these five components:

1. **Sentence-level rules.** Length distribution, contraction policy, whether
   fragments are allowed, how the brand handles lists.
2. **The lexicon.** Ten words the brand always uses, ten it never does, and
   the reason for each. Reasons matter: without them, people apply the list
   mechanically and produce parody.
3. **Paired examples.** Same message written on-voice and near-miss, with the
   difference annotated. Near-miss beats plain wrong — it teaches the edge.
4. **Register map.** How the voice shifts across contexts. A brand that
   sounds identical in a joke and in a safety notice has a costume, not a
   voice.
5. **Non-negotiables.** The handful of things that make it *this* brand and
   not a competitor. Usually three or four.

## Register map matters most at volume

Most off-brand work at scale is not wrong-voice, it is right-voice-wrong-room:
playful copy attached to a clinical statement, or clinical copy attached to a
community moment. Give the register map its own page and make it the first
thing a new editor reads.

For regulated categories, mark which registers may carry benefit language at
all. That boundary is a voice rule *and* a compliance control — see
`health-claim-gate`.

## Make it testable

A voice guide is only real if it can settle an argument. Build a short
calibration set: fifteen lines, some on-voice, some near-miss, answers
withheld. New editors, freelancers and agencies take it before their first
brief. Disagreement between the guide and an experienced editor is a signal
the guide is incomplete — update it rather than overruling by seniority.

## Feeding AI

The same artifact that makes freelancers accurate makes generated drafts
usable: lexicon, paired examples, and register map are exactly what a model
needs. Voice codification is therefore a prerequisite for AI-assisted volume,
not a parallel task. Undocumented voice is the single biggest reason
generated copy reads generic.

## Maintenance

Voice drifts through accumulated small compromises, not through a decision.
Review a random sample of shipped assets monthly against the calibration set.
When drift appears, decide explicitly whether the voice has evolved or slipped,
and update the guide either way.
