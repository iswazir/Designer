# Category Entry Points

Most performance creative targets a *person*. Better performance creative
targets a *moment* — the situation that puts someone in market at all.
Ehrenberg-Bass calls these category entry points: the cues that retrieve a
brand from memory when a need arises. Brands that get bought are the ones
attached to more moments than their rivals.

This matters operationally, not just strategically: entry points are the
best generator of non-duplicative creative concepts, which is what keeps a
large ad account from drowning in near-identical variations.

## Build the map

For each entry point capture five fields:

- **Trigger** — what happens in the world. ("Third bad night of sleep in a row.")
- **Where** — physical context. (Kitchen, 6:40am, before the kids are up.)
- **Emotional state** — what they feel in that second. (Resigned, not panicked.)
- **Current fix** — what they do today, including doing nothing.
- **Retrieval cue** — the sound, image, or phrase that could make the brand
  surface in that moment.

Aim for 15–25 entry points across the category. Fewer than ten means you
haven't left the office.

## Prioritise

Score each on three axes and plot them:

1. **Volume** — how often does this moment occur across the market?
2. **Winnability** — is a competitor already welded to it?
3. **Proof fit** — can you honestly claim relevance to this moment?

Proof fit is the axis that gets skipped and the one that causes trouble.
A high-volume moment your evidence cannot support is a trap: it will test
well, scale, and then collide with claims review. Check it early through
`health-claim-gate` rather than after a winner is already spending.

## Feed the engine

Entry points are the top of the concept funnel. Each one should generate:

- One lo-fi creator execution — the moment, filmed in the moment
- One studio or brand-film treatment — the moment, elevated
- One text-forward or static — the moment, named in words

That structure means one entry-point workshop produces a quarter of
genuinely distinct concepts rather than a spreadsheet of headline swaps.
Hand winners to `concept-brief` for execution-ready briefs, and let
`performance-creative-ladder` decide what graduates.

## Refresh cadence

Re-audit quarterly, and immediately after any category shock — a competitor
launch, a regulatory change, a cultural moment that reframes the need. Entry
points decay more slowly than creative, but they do decay.
