# Concept Brief

Rework is almost always a briefing failure, and it is the most expensive
failure in a content operation because it consumes capacity twice. At volume,
brief quality is throughput.

A good brief is one page. Two pages means the thinking isn't finished.

## The template

Use this structure. Its order matters: it front-loads what a maker needs to
start, and puts constraints where they'll be read rather than skimmed.

```
CONCEPT NAME
One line, memorable, used in every filename and dashboard downstream

THE ONE THING
The single idea a viewer should leave with. One sentence.

TENSION
The customer tension this sits on, in the customer's words

HOOK
The literal opening — words, action, or image. Written, not described.

PAYOFF
How the hook resolves. Must be answerable from the hook alone.

FORMAT + SPEC
Channel, ratio, duration, safe areas, deliverable count

CASTING / CONTEXT
Who is on screen, where, and why that person in that place

MUST INCLUDE
Claims-approved lines verbatim, mandatory disclosures, brand codes

MUST NOT
The specific traps for this concept, not generic don'ts

REFERENCE
One or two links, with a note on what to take from each

DONE LOOKS LIKE
The bar for a first pass, so revision rounds are predictable
```

## Fields people get wrong

**The one thing.** If it contains "and", the concept is two concepts and the
edit will feel muddy no matter who cuts it.

**Must include.** Claims language goes here *verbatim*, already cleared
through `health-claim-gate`. Paraphrasing an approved claim is how compliant
copy becomes non-compliant copy between brief and export.

**Must not.** Generic prohibitions get ignored. Concept-specific ones get
read — "don't let the powder look grainy on camera" beats "stay on brand".

**Reference.** One or two links, and say what to take from each. An
unannotated reference pile invites imitation of the wrong element.

**Done looks like.** Naming the first-pass bar is what makes a two-round
revision cycle achievable. Without it, editors guess at finish level and
either over-polish drafts or under-deliver finals.

## For high-stakes work

Brand film, out-of-home and ambassador shoots need three additions: the
approval chain with names and turnaround times, the talent's contractual
constraints, and the derivative plan — what this asset becomes downstream, so
it is shot to serve those uses. See `asset-multiplication`.

## Test before sending

Read the brief as if you were the editor. Can you start? If your first
instinct is to ask a question, answer it in the brief and send it then.
