### B2. AI evidence receipts, full detail (referenced from Slide 6)

**Receipt 1 — Concept branching**
- Input: approved product truth (one sachet replaces a multi-bottle routine) + audience tension (health improvement creates more to manage, not less) + claim constraint (no disease/fatigue/sleep/digestion/recovery/cognition claims; "one place to begin" is the only approved product-role line). Task: 6 hook lines for a 20–25s creator open.
- Output: (1) "I started trying to take care of myself. Somehow, I gave myself another thing I could fail at." (2) "My health routine had more steps than my actual job." (3) "I didn't need another system. I needed one thing I'd actually finish." (4) "Every app told me something different. None of them agreed with each other." (5) "I wasn't failing at my health. I was failing at my health admin." (6) "The hardest part of getting healthier was remembering to."
- Human edit: rejected #2 (generic workplace complaint, not health-specific), #4 (implies other apps/brands give bad advice — competitive-disparagement risk), #6 (too glib for the tension). Kept #1 as primary (shipped). Flagged #3 and #5 as second-wave test alternates.
- Value: one approved truth + tension pairing yields 6 testable opens in under a minute; net yield is 1 primary + 2 backups, human-selected.

**Receipt 2 — Claims/rights preflight (caught failure)**
- Input: draft line "By three, I'm reading the same line twice," reviewed against the Cognition & Focus claim territory (forbidden: brain fog, ADHD, cognitive decline, treat).
- Output: "FLAG — MEDIUM-HIGH RISK. No forbidden term used directly, but sequenced immediately before a product reveal it functions as an implied cognition/attention claim (symptom → product resolution = implied efficacy). Recommend cutting the direct causal sequencing, or replacing with a non-cognitive recognition beat."
- Human disposition: reviewer rejected the line outright — went further than the AI's own suggested fix, judging even the softened version too close to an attention-deficit implication. Redirected the tension through The Failure Trap's existing browser-tabs/admin-overload imagery instead.
- Why it matters: the human raised the bar past what the AI itself proposed — stronger evidence than "AI wrong, human right."

**Receipt 3 — Localization/transcreation**
- Input: approved English line "An ingredient list is not a formula," target market Germany, constraint: no claim inflation in translation.
- Output: "Eine Zutatenliste ist keine Formel." Flag: LOW RISK, meaning preserved, but "Formel" carries a slightly more clinical connotation in German consumer contexts than "formula" does in English — flagged for market review.
- Human disposition: market reviewer approved as-is, but added a supplement disclaimer line the German version needed that the English original doesn't.
- **Evidence label: analyst inference, not verified regulatory fact.** The specific German disclosure convention referenced is reasoned inference about that market's supplement advertising norms — have an actual German market reviewer confirm or replace before this ships.

## PART C — ALL FIVE CREATIVE CONCEPTS, FULL DETAIL

### 1. The Failure Trap
- Target: adults ~35–55 already trying to take care of their health, juggling trackers/apps/supplements/advice.
- Hook: "I started trying to take care of myself. Somehow, I gave myself another thing I could fail at."
- Tension: more health advice, protocols, and products create the feeling of falling behind, not getting ahead.
- Format/channel: creator 9:16 paid social, static, CRM, retail QR, AppLovin playable.
- Portfolio job: recognition/affinity. Ownership is the resolution device (quiet kitchen reset, phone face-down, one red sachet), not the tension.
- Claim flag: no disease/fatigue/sleep/digestion/recovery/cognition claim; no mocking multiple interventions; only approved product-role line is "One place to begin."
- First-scale bet (current).

**Storyboard** (9:16, ~20–25s):
1. Handheld phone-camera feel — blurred browser tabs, generic illegible health searches. "I started trying to take care of myself."
2. Cluttered cabinet, mismatched generic pill bottles. "Somehow, I gave myself another thing I could fail at."
3. Quick montage — app notification, saved wellness post, half-finished planner.
4. Cut to stillness — phone face-down on counter, morning window light.
5. Hands tear a plain foil sachet, pour into water, crimson bloom.
6. End card: "ONE PLACE TO BEGIN."

- Hook: "An ingredient list is not a formula."
- Tension: skeptical people want to know how to inspect what they're taking, not hear another promise.
- Format/channel: organic carousel, Reels, PDP proof hub, CRM, paid proof fragment.
- Portfolio job: proof reimagined. Ownership is the FORM→DOSE→PAIRING→EVIDENCE system, not a generic transparency claim.
- Claim flag: no "synergy," "clinically proven," "best absorbed," or product-specific dose/trial claim until the Approved Content Menu exists.

### 3. The Long Answer
- Target: curious, health-literate, higher-intent — podcast/YouTube audience.
- Hook: "Not every question should end with IM8. Every answer should leave people better informed than when they arrived."
- Tension: the first answer in wellness marketing is usually marketing; some people want the question behind it.
- Format/channel: YouTube, podcast, Healthspan Blog (canonical home), organic clips, paid question fragments, PR.
- Portfolio job: editorial authority. Ownership is the fixed format contract, not any single topic.
- Claim flag: locked episode "What does clinically tested actually mean?" is safe. Superpower blood-test episode is ACTIVE/NEEDS VALIDATION — needs privacy/partner/regulatory review; never imply the retest proves personal efficacy.

**Storyboard** (16:9 documentary, segment structure, not product-action frames):
1. Cold open — question on screen, no host: "What does clinically tested actually mean?"
2. Interview setup — formulation scientist in an ordinary office, natural light.
3. B-roll — two printed documents, illegible text, crimson marker underline.
4. Second viewpoint — independent voice, complicating read.
5. Card: "WHAT THIS SHOWS. WHAT IT DOES NOT."
6. Close — narrator poses the next better question, Healthspan Blog card.
