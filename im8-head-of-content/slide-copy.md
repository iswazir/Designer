## Slide 4

**Eyebrow:** THE MACHINE · 04 of 12

### On slide

**Title:** One Loop. Two Gates.

**Subtitle:** Every brief moves from signal to learning. Review depth rises with consequence.

**Loop (four phases):**

1. **Frame.** Signal → Intake and triage. Is there an owner, outcome, source, deadline, risk level, and approval path?
2. **Make.** Production → **Gate 1.** Is this good, and is it IM8?
3. **Adapt.** Version and localize → **Gate 2.** Is this version shippable in this market?
4. **Release and learn.** Signal out → Response in → Measure → Learn. What should the next signal become?

**Gate labels:** Gate 1: accountable creative owner. Gate 2: release owner with domain approvers.

**Two accent paths through the same loop:**

- **Fast and reversible.** Approved patterns, automated preflight, lead-editor release, sampled senior audit.
- **High stakes.** Early senior direction, formal domain review, market and talent approval, controlled release.

**Handoff line:** Content hands off the market-ready asset. Channel owners deploy it.

Word count: 104.

### Production

Breadcrumb: a cropped master and two localized versions moving through Gate 1 and Gate 2. Stage-by-stage RACI goes to appendix A3 and A4.

### Loom

Gate 1 owns concept integrity, creative standard, source accuracy, master quality, and strategic fit. Gate 2 owns claim clearance, rights, market meaning, language, accessibility, and technical release. Adaptation and Localization never owns distribution. Handoff: the loop only moves this fast if judgment is delegated safely rather than centralized.

## Slide 5

**Eyebrow:** THE MACHINE · 05 of 12

### On slide

**Title:** Judgment Scales.

**Subtitle:** Standards get faster when editors learn to make the call, not wait for one.

**Authority ladder (four rungs, bottom to top):**

- **AI preflight.** Technical checks, approved-claim matching, metadata, expiry flags. Escalates on low confidence, conflicting sources, or a failed hard rule.
- **Editor.** Self-QA, platform craft, source and version accuracy. Escalates outside approved patterns or on uncertain claims and rights.
- **Lead editor.** Routine creative release, coaching, calibration. Escalates high-cost, permanent, talent-sensitive, or defining work.
- **Head of Content.** Flagship judgment, new territory, portfolio coherence, sampled audits.

**Non-delegable boundary (outside the ladder):** Science, Legal, Regulatory, Market, and Talent give final approval in their domains. AI never infers it.

**Growth loop:** Make → Review → Measure → Diagnose → Coach → Certify more authority

**Management strip:** Level: scope of judgment · Coach: the observed gap · Protect: craft time · Grow: larger problems, not more assets

**First three hires (proposed):**
1. **Creative Operations Lead.** Removes workflow, capacity, DAM, and rights friction across every discipline.
2. **Performance Creative Lead.** Turns the largest growth demand into better hypotheses and faster learning.
3. **Adaptation Lead.** Makes 43-market readiness an owned capability, not a late production step.

Assumes the inherited team has craft but lacks senior ownership and operations. If the day-10 audit shows a flagship craft gap, hire a Lead Editor third instead.

Word count: 168. Over budget. Fix in layout, not by cutting the hires: set the four rungs as label plus one short line, move each rung's escalation trigger into a hover-style caption strip, and let the ladder carry the visual weight.

### Production

Breadcrumb: one annotated review frame showing an approved-versus-escalated example. Great week, onboarding, editor levels, Cutting Room Review, underperformance, retention, and burnout controls go to appendix A5 and A6.

### Loom

Two spoken judgments: no editor is planned at 100 percent utilization, because that plan has already built in quality debt, rework, and burnout; and a senior editor should not only make better work, they should make the people around them better. Editors advance by earning decision rights over tasks, then packages, then franchises, then people and systems. Handoff: once judgment is explicit and owned, AI can safely extend the team's reach.

## Slide 10

**Eyebrow:** THE WORK · 10 of 12

### On slide

**Title:** Convert Now.

**Subtitle:** Fast, platform-native concepts turn live customer tensions into immediate tests.

Both concepts use the same card anatomy: Name · Target · Tension · Hook · Format · Storyboard · Proof and CTA · What we learn.

#### Concept 1: One Person. One Problem.

**Target:** Adults 30 to 55 who already take something every morning and still hit the same wall by mid-afternoon. Insight hypothesis; validate in comments and support themes before scaling.

**Tension:** People do not want more energy. They want the energy they have to last.

**Hook (spoken to camera, first two seconds):** "I don't need more energy. I need mine to last past three."

**Format:** 9:16 creator documentary, 20 to 30 seconds, sound-on with captions, natural light, no studio.

**Storyboard:**
1. 3:02 p.m. Desk. The slump nobody films.
2. The hook line, to camera, unpolished.
3. Next morning. One scoop, one glass, no ceremony.
4. Day 30. Same desk, same time. "Still one scoop."
5. Pack. "90 ingredients. One scoop." CTA.

**Proof and CTA:** 90 ingredients in one daily scoop. Approved claim wording per market. CTA: Start your first 30 days.

**What we learn:** Which tension territory (energy, sleep, focus, digestion, hydration) earns the best cycle-two retention, not just the cheapest click.

#### Concept 2: Out of the Green

**Target:** Category-aware buyers who assume every daily powder is the same green scoop.

**Tension:** A whole category shares one color. Nobody has claimed the other one.

**Hook (headline on a full IM8-red field):** "Everything else is green."

**Format:** 6-second social reveal, paid static, motion poster. OOH-capable master.

**Storyboard:**
1. A wall of green scoops, anonymous, identical.
2. One red scoop enters frame.
3. The whole field turns red. Headline.
4. Pack. Endline: Out of the Green.

**Proof and CTA:** Product legible in every frame. "90 ingredients. One scoop." CTA: See what's in the red one.

**What we learn:** Does distinctive-asset recognition rise while CAC holds? First test: red-field static versus current control, matched audiences, brand-lift read at 30 days.

Word count: 268 across two full concept cards. Concept pages may run long because hooks and frames are the proof; keep every field to one line and let the storyboard frames carry the rest.

## Slide 11

**Eyebrow:** THE WORK · 11 of 12

### On slide

**Title:** Build Belief.

**Subtitle:** Product and science become compelling when proof resolves a real human doubt.

#### Concept 3: Proof, Translated.

**Target:** Evidence-curious buyers who distrust "clinically proven" because nobody shows them what was measured.

**Tension:** A study is not a claim. Most ads pretend it is.

**Hook (card 1):** "A study is not a claim. Here's the difference."

**Format:** Six-card editorial carousel. Reel cut, PDP proof card, Healthspan Blog graphic.

**Storyboard (cards):**
1. The question.
2. What was measured, and on whom.
3. What that lets us say.
4. What it does not let us say.
5. Where IM8 sits, with the exact approved claim.
6. Read the full answer.

**Proof and CTA:** Approved study or certification language, cited, per market. CTA: Read the full answer.

**What we learn:** Does evidence-boundary honesty convert as well as standard proof ads while lowering refunds and cancellations within two cycles?

#### Concept 4: The Long Answer

**Episode 01:** Can you actually measure whether this works?

**Target:** Subscribers and considerers who want a health worldview, not a product FAQ.

**Tension:** Everyone says "it works." Almost nobody says how you would know.

**Hook (Instagram question Reel):** "Can you actually measure whether this works?"

**Format:** Instagram question Reel → YouTube episode, podcast, canonical Healthspan Blog article, clips, CRM.

**Spine:** Baseline 100+ biomarkers → 90 days of real life → Same-method retest → Honest interpretation

**Closing frame:** What changed. What did not. What we still cannot claim.

**Proof and offer:** Superpower supplies the measurement infrastructure, not causal proof. Preferred access for eligible subscribers.

**What we learn:** Repeat consumption and article-to-email conversion, plus retest completion against cycle-two retention.

Word count: 242 across two concept cards.

Individual before-and-after results need consent, privacy controls, and language that does not imply a controlled efficacy result. ### Loom

## Slide 12

**Eyebrow:** THE WORK · 12 of 12

### On slide

**Title:** Create Memory.

**Subtitle:** A flagship ambassador idea becomes repeatable brand IP, not a one-off endorsement.

#### Concept 5: What Are You Training For?

**Premise:** One question, asked of everyone. The answer is never a trophy.

**Target:** People who train for a life, not a podium, and the fans who assume Beckham only ever trained for football.

**Tension:** The category sells performance to people who are not competing in anything.

**Hook:** "What are you training for?"

**Format:** :60 CTV film, :30 social, :15 cutdowns, OOH portraits. Then creator and customer answers.

**Storyboard:**
1. Dawn. Empty pitch. Beckham alone, no crowd, no kit sponsor in frame.
2. Voice: "What are you training for?"
3. Cut: a woman mid-lift in a garage gym. A founder on a 6 a.m. call. A nurse ending a night shift. A 68-year-old on a road bike.
4. Beckham's answer, plain, to camera. Proposed line, subject to talent approval: "Not a final. Tuesday."
5. The scoop. The same one, for all of them.
6. Endline: What are you training for? IM8.

**Franchise:** The question becomes a recurring format. Ambassador answers, creator answers, customer answers from a Story prompt. Every answer is an asset family.

**Why these five:** See me → Make it unmistakably IM8 → Help me understand → Give me reason to believe → Give the brand a meaning I want to join. Five jobs, five proof structures, five visual worlds. No two share a tension, a cast, or a proof.

**First-scale bet (the deck's only high-emphasis accent panel):**
**One Person. One Problem.** Cheapest to learn, fastest to read, and it tells the other four which tensions are real. Disconfirm it if clicks win but cycle-two retention trails control: the territories are recruiting low-fit customers, and budget moves to Proof, Translated.

**Closing line:** The machine ships 5,000 assets. The system makes each one build the next advantage.
