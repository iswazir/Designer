# Founder Signal

A famous face buys attention, not belief. The brands that convert it —
Skims, Rhode, Fenty — do something more specific than borrowing fame: they
make the person's *judgement* the product's evidence. The claim is not "she
is famous", it is "she is exacting about this, and here is what that
produced."

Brands that skip this step get a spike, a plateau, and a permanent
dependency.

## The three uses of a face

Decide which one you are buying. They need different content, different shoot
plans, and different volumes.

1. **Attention** — the face makes people stop. Cheapest use, shortest
   half-life, and the one most campaigns default to.
2. **Credibility transfer** — the person's domain expertise vouches for the
   product. Only works where the domain matches: an athlete on recovery is
   credible; the same athlete on skincare is decoration.
3. **Standard-setting** — the person defines the quality bar. Highest value,
   requires genuine involvement, and cannot be faked for long.

## Make judgement visible

The mechanism that converts fame into belief is showing the person choosing,
rejecting, or insisting. Not holding the product — *deciding* about it.

Practically, this means shooting the decision: the taste test they didn't
like, the formulation argument, the specific thing they demanded. It requires
real access, and it is the reason ambassador shoot windows should be planned
around capturing judgement rather than only capturing beauty coverage.

## Protect against collapse

A brand fully fused to one person inherits their risk and cannot outgrow
them. Three structural defences:

- **Own distinctive assets independent of the face** — see
  `distinctive-assets`. The brand must be recognisable when the person isn't
  in frame.
- **Maintain a non-talent creative line** — a meaningful share of always-on
  performance work should never feature the ambassador, both to prove the
  proposition stands alone and to keep a fallback tested and warm.
- **Diversify the roster deliberately** — several credible faces in different
  domains reduce single-person exposure without diluting, provided each one
  is used for credibility transfer in their own domain.

## Compliance under a famous face

Endorsement rules apply regardless of stature, and a founder is not exempt
from them. Two things reliably cause trouble: personal-experience language
that becomes an implied benefit claim, and disclosure that is technically
present but not clear and conspicuous. A high-profile endorser raises
scrutiny rather than lowering it — see `health-claim-gate` and treat talent
scripts as claims-bearing copy, because that is what they are.

## Operating constraints

Talent time is the scarcest input in the system. Treat shoot windows as
immovable and plan the rest of the calendar around them, with the derivative
brief written *before* the shoot so the day serves every downstream channel.
`asset-multiplication` covers that mechanics; `tentpole-allocation` covers
what gets displaced to make room.
